# The Memory Repairman — World Bible

## Premise

In the near future, human memory can be extracted into a portable format called an **engram** — a lossy, editable recording of a specific recollection. Engrams can be played back (a "replay"), shared, archived, and, critically, **repaired**.

Repair is not restoration. When an engram is damaged — by trauma, illness, decay, or deliberate erasure — the gaps cannot be recovered. They are filled with **synthetic connective tissue**: plausible, generated material that smooths the fracture. The facts survive. The feeling does not.

The industry slogan: *"We give you back what happened."* The unspoken truth among repairmen: *"We give you back the shape of what happened."*

## The Loom

The tool of the trade is a **loom** — a table-sized apparatus that renders an engram as a navigable structure. A repairman works inside it the way a conservator works on a fresco: stabilizing cracks, keying in fill, deciding what is original and what is invention. Every repair leaves a seam. Most clients never notice. Some do, and come back.

## The Cost

Synthetic fill is stable but inert. It carries no emotional weight. A repaired memory of a wedding still shows the faces, the light, the words — but the warmth is gone, replaced by a clean, documentary calm. Heavy repair produces what repairmen call **"the grey"**: a person whose past is intact and hollow. There is no clinical name for it, because the companies that sell repair have no interest in naming it.

## The Protagonist

**Elias Vane**, late fifties, was once a memory architect — one of the engineers who designed the first commercial looms. He left the industry after an incident he never discusses and now runs a small, unlicensed repair shop on Cinder Lane in the Ward, taking the jobs the official clinics refuse: the legally ambiguous, the quietly desperate.

He has a rule: he does not repair his own engrams. He knows exactly what that rule costs him.

## Core Conflict

A client arrives with an unlabeled engram, paid in advance, marked only *"for E. Vane."* It is Elias's own — a memory he deliberately erased years ago, the one thing his rule was built to protect him from. To repair it is to break his rule and learn what he chose to forget. To refuse is to keep living inside a decision he can no longer remember making.

## Themes

- What makes a memory *yours*: the facts, or the feeling?
- Whether a comfortable lie is kinder than a painful truth.
- The ethics of "helping" by erasing.

## Recurring Motifs

- The smell of rain on hot metal.
- A blue door that appears in more than one memory.
- The phrase: *"You can save the facts. You can't save the feeling."*

## Voice & Style Notes

- Close third person, anchored in Elias's sensory experience.
- Restrained, precise prose. No cyberpunk neon spectacle — the world is worn, bureaucratic, mundane.
- Dialogue does the thematic work; exposition arrives through action and objects.
- Chapter endings turn on a small, concrete discovery rather than a cliffhanger.
