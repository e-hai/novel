# The Memory Repairman — Act II Outline (Chapters 4–12)

The first three chapters form a complete movement: Elias learns what he erased and refuses the comfortable fill. Act II asks the harder question — **now that he knows, how does he live and work?** The arc moves from private reckoning to public consequence: the repair trade, the grey, the company that made the looms, and the people who pay to forget.

Continuity rules (from world bible): close third person on Elias; restrained, bureaucratic, worn world; no neon spectacle; dialogue carries theme; endings turn on a small concrete discovery, not a cliffhanger; motifs recur (rain on hot metal / blue door / "you can save the facts, you can't save the feeling"). Target ~800–1,000 words per chapter for a sustainable daily cadence — shorter than Act I, but each still a complete beat.

## Chapter 4 — "The Woman Who Kept Receipts"
**Function:** Show Elias working with his new knowledge; introduce a client whose problem is the opposite of forgetting.
- A woman brings an engram of a marriage she wants *un*-repaired — she had it smoothed years ago and now cannot feel any of it.
- Elias faces the irony: clients pay to remove feeling; this one pays to get it back, and it cannot be recovered.
- He can only tell her the truth he just learned himself: the shape is there, the warmth is gone.
- **End beat:** She leaves unchanged, but asks to leave the envelope "for the next one" — a small echo of Ch.1's sealed envelope.

## Chapter 5 — "A Blue Door in Someone Else's Memory"
**Function:** Escalate the motif into a mystery; the blue door is not only in Elias's past.
- A young man's damaged engram shows the same blue door, in a building neither of them knows.
- Elias realizes the door is not a place — it is a design signature, a "brand" left by the company's early looms.
- Suggests the erasure of Elias's memory was not self-inflicted alone; someone else was in the room.
- **End beat:** Elias finds a serial number etched inside his own loom that he never noticed before.

## Chapter 6 — "What the Grey Remember"
**Function:** Give the "grey" a voice; broaden from private to social cost.
- Elias is asked to repair a former colleague — heavily grey, facts intact, hollow — at the colleague's family's request.
- In the loom, the colleague's memory is clean and documentary: a life with no seams because everything was smoothed.
- The family wanted "him back"; Elias must explain there is no original left to restore.
- **End beat:** The colleague's calm voice says the slogan — "We give you back what happened" — without any sense of the lie.

## Chapter 7 — "The Clinic on the Corner"
**Function:** Introduce the institutional antagonist without a villain caricature.
- Elias passes a licensed clinic advertising "same-day repair"; a poster shows a smiling, feelingless family.
- He recognizes the architect of the first commercial looms — his former partner, now running the clinic — on a dated consultation recording.
- A quiet confrontation: the partner offers Elias a place back, in exchange for his silence about the grey.
- **End beat:** Elias declines, and the partner says, gently, "You always did confuse honesty with kindness."

## Chapter 8 — "The Engram That Would Not Close"
**Function:** A technical spine to the moral one; show the craft.
- A damaged engram arrives that repairs keep reopening — the synthetic fill rejects, the seam will not hold.
- Elias discovers it contains two memories woven together: a mother's and a child's, from the same moment.
- Repairing one destroys the other. He must choose which to save, knowing the choice is his to make and not neutral.
- **End beat:** He saves the child's, and records the mother's in the drawer with his own rule — the second unrepairable engram.

## Chapter 9 — "Mara, Again"
**Function:** Resolve the mentor thread; personal stakes.
- Mara (the voice from Ch.2, Elias's former mentor) appears in person — older, greyer, now a client rather than a ghost.
- She asks him to repair her own memory of the incident, the one that made her leave the industry too.
- Reveal: Mara was in the room when Elias's memory was erased, and she did it — to protect him, or herself, or both.
- **End beat:** She does not ask forgiveness. She asks him to decide what the truth is worth, now that he has it.

## Chapter 10 — "The Rule, Written Down"
**Function:** Theme made literal; Elias rewrites his rule.
- Elias re-reads the rule he wrote and placed in the drawer in Ch.3 — "Do not repair your own engrams."
- He realizes the rule was not protection but avoidance; the fracture he left raw in Ch.3 can be lived with, not just preserved.
- He does not repair himself. He changes the rule: he will work on his own memory only in the presence of another witness, never alone.
- **End beat:** He asks Mara to sit with him at the loom — the first shared repair.

## Chapter 11 — "Two at the Loom"
**Function:** Climax of Act II; shared repair as answer to isolation.
- Elias and Mara open the sealed engram together — the erased incident, seen from two sides.
- The truth is neither innocence nor guilt: the technology was sold knowing the grey would come, and both signed.
- For the first time, the fill is not synthetic — it is another person's witness, imperfect and feeling.
- **End beat:** The seam holds. Not clean, but shared. The blue door opens onto an ordinary room.

## Chapter 12 — "Tell Me What She Actually Did"
**Function:** Return to the opening; show change through repetition.
- The old man from Ch.1 returns again, as in Ch.3's end beat, wanting his wife "fixed" once more.
- This time Elias does the repair differently: he refuses to smooth the seams, and asks the old man to tell the story, not the facts.
- The old man begins to speak — imperfect, feeling, alive. The chapter ends mid-sentence, the first open ending.
- **End beat:** Elias leaves the drawer closed. The sign on the shop still says nothing. The rain still smells like hot metal.
