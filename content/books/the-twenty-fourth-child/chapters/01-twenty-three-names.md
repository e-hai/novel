# Twenty-Three Names

Sallow Green had twenty-three children on its roll and a school built for forty.

Nell Darrow was the third teacher in four years. The village told her that before it told her the weather, and the weather was the only thing the village liked talking about more.

The school was one room with two windows, a stove that drank coal, and a cupboard behind the door where the attendance register lived. The register had an oilcloth cover and a stamp on the front that said SALLOW GREEN CHURCH SCHOOL, and inside, ruled in somebody's steady hand, columns for names, for mornings, for afternoons, and a narrow one at the end with no heading at all.

The names went back to 1874. There were twenty-three of them on the current page, in no order Nell could work out, and she read them aloud on her first morning the way she had been taught to read them: surname first, then the Christian name, then wait.

Twenty-three children answered. She ticked twenty-three boxes and looked up, and there were twenty-four of them in the room.

She counted again, moving her lips, and got twenty-four. She counted at lunch and got twenty-three. She counted at three o'clock, when the light came in flat off the fields, and got twenty-four again, and the number sat in her chest like a swallowed stone.

There were explanations. There was a child from the next parish who came for the stove. There was a door left open. There was the fact that she was new, and tired, and had counted the same boy twice because he had moved.

She went to the register and opened it at the last page with writing on it. The narrow column had a number in it for every day of that week and every week before it. Twenty-four, twenty-four, twenty-four, going back up the page in six different inks.

Nell put the cap back on her pen and wrote twenty-four in the column, because that was the number of children in the room, and a register that disagreed with the room was worse than a register that disagreed with itself.
