# A Copy of a Page

Every September the register was copied into a new book, and the old one went into the cupboard, and the copied page carried the same totals forward. Nell found the instruction pasted inside the front cover, in the same hand as the stamp: *Copy the roll on the first Monday. Do not copy the marks. Enter the totals.*

Somebody had copied the marks.

On the last endpaper, under the pasted instruction, there was a line of writing in brown ink, small and upright, dated the fourteenth of November 1911, and signed with two initials.

*This page is a copy. The original was signed by a person who was not in the room.*

Nell sat with that for a while. She had a good mind for paper. She had been trained to read a register as a record of what had been written, and to distrust any page that could not say who had written it and when. The line on the endpaper said exactly what was wrong with the book: not that it had lied, but that it had copied. Twenty-three names, twenty-four marks, and behind both of them a signature sheet signed in a room where the person signing was not present to be seen.

She spent the afternoon comparing September pages. Every copy since 1911 had twenty-four marks. Every copy had the extra mark in the same place: one line below *Tull, R.*, above *Wray, A.*, in the column for mornings, in a space where no name was written.

In the last week of term a woman came to the school. She wore a good coat and carried a leather case and said her name was Fenn, and that she was a clerk, and she was apologetic about the timing in a way that suggested she had been apologetic in a great many schoolrooms.

"I have a file," Miss Fenn said, "opened in 1911, closed in 1911, and reopened this month because a teacher in Sallow Green has been writing totals that agree with the room." She took out a photograph of a page. "A duplicate of a signature, made by a person who was not present. Filed under a number the county never issued. The office exists for events with no witness, Miss Darrow. This one has a document."

"What does the office want?"

"To know whether the mark has a name yet."
