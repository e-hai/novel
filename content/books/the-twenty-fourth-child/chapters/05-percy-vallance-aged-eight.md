# Percy Vallance, Aged Eight

The parish register was kept in the church safe, and the vicar let her read it on a Tuesday afternoon with the door open because the safe was old and the lock was older.

*Vallance, Percy. Son of Thomas and Ada Vallance of this parish. Buried the fourth of March 1911. Aged eight years. Diphtheria.*

Nell read it twice and then went back through the school logbook. The school had closed on the twenty-eighth of February and reopened on the third of April. Three children had been ill. Two had gone north. Percy Vallance was not mentioned in the log at all, in the years he would have been at the school, in the weeks of the illness, or in the week of the funeral.

He was absent from the school's record so completely that his absence had a shape: a space on the roll that was exactly one name wide, and a mark in the column that had been made for him every morning for a hundred and eleven years by whoever kept the account.

She asked Miss Petty whether the name meant anything. Miss Petty put down her sewing and said, "There was a Vallance on the hill. The family went. It was a long time ago." Then she picked the sewing up again and did not look at Nell for the rest of the afternoon.

That evening Nell took the register out of the cupboard and set it on the desk under the lamp. She ruled a line in the margin, beside the empty column beside the empty space, and she wrote, in her own hand, the way the office wrote everything: the date at the top, then the name.

*26th November. Vallance, Percy. Aged eight. Present.*

She did not know why she wrote present. It was the only column the book had.

In the morning she read the roll. Twenty-three children answered, and she read the name in the margin as well, and this time the answer came at once, from the back, from the boy in the coat: "Here." And Ivy Mace, without looking up from her sum, said, "He's got a name now," in the voice of a child reporting that the rain had stopped.
