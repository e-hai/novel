# The Mark Retired

The county took eleven weeks to answer. When the answer came it was one line and a form.

*The roll is amended to twenty-four names as of 26 November. The grant is adjusted from the date of amendment. No recovery is sought for prior years.*

Nell read the last sentence several times. Somebody in an office had decided that a hundred and eleven years of a penny a day was not worth pursuing, and had decided it in a way that made the decision look like generosity rather than arithmetic.

She filled in the new return. Twenty-four on roll. Average attendance twenty-four. Sum claimed, corrected, and a note in the box at the bottom, because the box said *Remarks* and she had remarks.

*One name on the roll, Vallance, P., was recorded present from 1911 to 2026 following a copy of a page signed in his absence. He was buried at Sallow Green on 4 March 1911, aged eight.*

Miss Fenn came once more, in December, with the case and the file. She read the remark and copied it out in her own hand, which was small and upright, and then she let Nell look at the file. It was thin. There was the photograph of the page. There was a copy of the 1911 letter and the reply. There was a sheet of paper headed with a number and the words *Case seven*, and under it, in the same small hand, a single sentence: *An event without a witness, kept in account for one hundred and eleven years by a mark that nobody signed.*

"There is nothing else to put in it," Miss Fenn said. "You could put the boy's name. That is what the office would do."

So Nell wrote it, and Miss Fenn took the file away, and the school went on.

The boy in the coat did not come back after the Christmas holiday. For a week the chair by the window was warm in the mornings. Then it was cold, and stayed cold, and in March Nell moved it to the wall and put a plant on it.

The register went to the county archive at the end of the summer term, because a new book had been ordered and the old one was full. On the last page, in the column with no heading, there were twenty-four marks for every day of that term, and on the facing page, in the margin, a name and a date in a hand that the office could identify.

Her own.
