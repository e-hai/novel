# The Three O'Clock Run

The three o'clock run was the one nobody watched. Ada Kell had been doing it for two years and had never once been asked what happened during it.

The model came in at 02:58, chattering down the line from the regional centre, and by 03:00 the station list had settled. Thirty-one reporting stations across the western district, each with a wind vector, a dew point, a rainfall total for the last hour.

She read them the way you read a shopping list. Then she read them again, because one of the names was wrong.

HALLOWAY. Population eleven hundred and forty. Rainfall, last hour: 2.4 millimetres.

Ada moved the cursor down the column and back up. Thirty-one stations. Thirty-two names. She counted twice more, which is what a person does at three in the morning when there is no one to ask.

"Paul," she said into the phone, at ten past three, because Paul was the duty forecaster and duty forecasters were paid to be awake.

"Mm."

"The model's dumping a station that isn't in the gazetteer. Halloway."

A pause, the length of a man sitting up. "Spell it."

"Halloway. Two four millimetres. Wind south-west, four knots."

"Never heard of it. If it's in the output, it's in the input." He yawned, badly. "Ask the hydrology lot in the morning. And don't file it, Ada, it'll just come back at us from QA."

She did not file it. She wrote it on the back of a printed sheet, with the time, and put the sheet in her bag, which was the one act of the night that could not be explained by procedure.

The rain outside her window was doing what the model said it would do: two millimetres, then a stop, then more from the west.

At four, the automatic gate at the reservoir logged an inch. At five, the river gauge at Carrow Bridge logged twenty-eight millimetres in the hour. Ada looked at both numbers and then at Halloway's, which she had written down in biro: 2.4.

The correct figure for Carrow Bridge should have been twenty-eight. It was 2.8. She had copied it wrong, and had been looking for a mistake in the model when the mistake was in her own hand.

Except that Halloway's figure had been too small by exactly the factor of ten as well, and Halloway's name had still not been on any list she had ever been given.

She stayed until seven, and then went home, and slept four hours, and at eleven the following morning she typed the word into the office system's search box and got nothing at all.
