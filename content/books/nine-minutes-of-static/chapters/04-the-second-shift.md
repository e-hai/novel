# The Second Shift

On the eleventh day the burst was eight minutes long.

Ivo measured it against the chronometer three times. The tape began at 03:12 and the hiss stopped at 03:20, and the voice inside it counted from eight, not nine, and did not reach zero.

“It’s losing a minute a day,” Deol said.

“Or gaining one.”

“Losing.”

Ivo wrote it in the log and did not sleep.

They checked the clock. The station clock was four seconds slow, then eleven, then forty. The chronometer was steady. The clock was not. On Kestrel the clock ran the lights and the clock ran the water and the clock ran the roster, and a clock that was eleven seconds slow was a station that would eventually be wrong about what time it was.

Mira came to the comms room with bread and stayed to listen.

“It sounds like someone reading a list,” she said.

“It sounds like someone counting.”

“Same thing, out here.” She looked at the tape spools. “When it gets to zero, what happens?”

Nobody answered her.

That night Ivo went to the dark deck. It was not on any current diagram, so he walked the corridor until the corridor ended in a bulkhead with no handle, and put his hand on it, and felt it warm.

Bulkheads on Kestrel were cold. Everything on Kestrel was cold. This one had a heartbeat in it, faint and regular, and when he counted the beats between them he got eight.

He went back to the comms room and wrote in the log:

*Bulkhead warm. Eight-second pulse. The count is not the burst. The count is us.*

Then he added, because it was true, and because the log was the only place he could say it:

*When it reaches zero I think the station stops being able to hear anything at all.*
