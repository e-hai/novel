# Nine Minutes of Static

The station was called Kestrel, and it was old enough that its name had gone out of date.

Ivo Sarn had been its communications officer for four years, which on Kestrel made him the second-longest-serving person aboard. The first was the engineer, Deol, who had been there eleven and did not like to be reminded of it.

Their job was to listen. Kestrel sat at the edge of the shipping lanes, and every hour it swept the band and logged what came back: weather packets, cargo chatter, the long deep hum of nothing.

At 03:12 station time, the sweep returned a burst of static nine minutes long.

Ivo logged it as interference and went to make tea.

He was pouring when the burst repeated, and this time he heard something inside it.

A voice. Not a word, not a call sign, just a voice, at the low end of the band, counting: *nine. eight. seven.*

He stopped the tape and played it back at half speed. The counting was still there, and it was still going down.

“Deol,” he said on the intercom. “Listen to this.”

Deol listened for a long time. Then he said, “Where is it coming from?”

“Inside the burst.”

“Bursts don’t have insides.”

“This one does.”

They ran the direction finder twice. It gave them the array itself.

Ivo looked at the numbers for a while and then wrote in the log, in the box marked *unexplained*, which he had used four times in four years:

*03:1. Nine minutes of static. Voice within. Counts down from nine. Source: local.*

Underneath, because he was honest, he wrote: *I do not want to log this twice.*
