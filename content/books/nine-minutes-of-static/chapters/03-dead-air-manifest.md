# Dead Air Manifest

The old deck plan was in the engineering locker, rolled in a tube that smelled of oil.

Ivo spread it on the galley table at night, when Mira had gone to her bunk and the station had the particular quiet that came after the third sweep.

Kestrel had been built with seven modules. The plan showed six. Between the array and the water plant there was a rectangle with no label, hatched in grey, marked *M-3*.

“That’s the dark deck,” Deol said. “Everyone knows about the dark deck.”

“What happened to it?”

“It failed. They sealed it.”

“Failed how?”

Deol drank his tea and did not answer, which on Kestrel meant the answer was in the log room and he did not want to go and get it.

Ivo went. The log room was cold and smelled of paper, and the shelf he wanted was the one nobody had touched since the station was commissioned. The volumes were stacked flat, unindexed, and heavy.

He found it in the fourth volume: nineteen years earlier, a maintenance entry, then a silence, then a line in a different hand.

*M-3 atmosphere compromised. Two crew inside. Seal ordered. Seal executed.*

Under it, small, as if the writer had been tired:

*One crew recovered.*

He read it four times. Two inside. One recovered. The third name, the one that had gone into the paperwork and been completed for him, was in the disembarkation column with a date and a shuttle number that did not match any shuttle in the port records.

Ivo photographed the page and put the volume back exactly where it had been.

Then he went to the array room and stood in the cold with his hand on the cable housing, and said, out loud, to the nine minutes of hiss that had not yet arrived:

“Anselm. If that’s you, I can hear you. Tell me what you need.”

The array hummed. The clock on the wall turned over to the hour.

Nothing came.

At 03:12, the burst came, and the voice said: *A door.*
