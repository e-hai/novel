# The Orchard in November

Father Cade came to Orra in November, which the village said was the wrong month for a new priest and the right month for everything else.

There were forty-one people in Orra and a church with a leaking roof and an orchard behind it that no one had pruned in living memory.

The orchard was the first thing he saw. He came up the lane with his case in his hand, and the trees stood in rows on the slope behind the village, bare of leaves and heavy with something that was not fruit.

He put his case down at the gate and looked.

The things on the branches were white and smooth and the size of a fist. Some were smaller. Some were the size of a child’s forearm and had a knob at one end and a socket at the other.

He went into the church and did not say anything about it, because a priest who arrives and immediately talks about the orchard is a priest who has arrived to talk about the orchard.

Mrs. Tull, who kept the church keys, gave him bread and a room and a list of the sick.

“The orchard,” he said, at the end, because he was not a coward.

“The orchard,” she said.

“What is it for?”

She looked at him for a moment with an expression he would come to know well: the look of a person deciding how much of a thing to say to a man who has just arrived.

“It’s for what we can’t bury,” she said, and took the lamp away with her, and left him in the dark with the list.
