# Address Not Found

Sunday, 11:37. Dell stood on Marlow Quay with the parcel under his arm and the scanner in his pocket.

The gap between 12 and 16 was a gap. He could see the skip, the wall, the weeds growing up the wall in a line like a signature.

At 11:39, a light came on in the gap.

It was not a light you could see from the outside of the street; it was a light you could see if you were the kind of person who was looking at 11:39. A window, second floor, yellow, old. Then another window. Then a door, at ground level, propped open with a brick.

At 11:40 exactly, number 14 was there.

It was a narrow building, four floors, more brick than glass, and it had a lift with a folding gate and a slot in the door for mail. Dell walked in. The lift took him up. It was slow in the way old lifts are slow, and he had time to think about what he was going to say to a brother he had buried.

The fourth floor corridor had one door, painted green, with the paint worn to wood at the handle.

He knocked. Nothing.

He knocked again. Then he put the parcel down on the mat, and took out the scanner, and did what a courier does, which is to serve the address and not the person.

He scanned the label. The scanner buzzed, once, and printed a receipt, and the receipt said, in the small grey letters at the bottom:

*DELIVERED. RECIPIENT PRESENT.*

Dell looked at the receipt, and then at the door, which was still shut, and then at the corridor, where the light was beginning to change, the way light changes when a window is closing.

The lift gate was already folding shut. He ran.
