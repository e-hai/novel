# The Package That Weighs Nothing

The voice on the phone was Dell’s brother.

Marco had been dead for six years. Dell had been at the funeral, had stood in the rain with his mother, had watched them put the coffin on the belt and had thought, absurdly, that his brother would have hated the music.

“Dell,” Marco said. “You took the parcel.”

“Marco.”

“Don’t do the voice. It takes too long.” Marco’s voice was the same, and tired in the same place. “Listen. You have to take it back.”

“You’re dead.”

“I’m not, actually, that’s the thing I’m trying to tell you.” There was a pause, and the sound of a street. “I’m on the quay. Number 14 is there. It’s there every Sunday. It isn’t there Monday.”

Dell sat down on the kitchen floor with the phone against his ear.

“Where have you been?” he said.

“In a room,” Marco said. “On the fourth floor. With the parcel you’re holding. I sent it to you because I can’t leave, and you can, and because the address only exists on Sundays and you’re the only person I know who works Sundays.”

“Six years.”

“Six years.” Marco’s voice cracked on it. “Dell, I think I’ve been in that room since the accident. I don’t know how many Sundays. I stopped counting. The tape clicks and it’s Sunday again and I’m back in the room.”

The parcel was on the table, empty now except for the phone. Dell looked at it for a long time.

“What do I do?”

“Deliver it,” Marco said. “To 14. Before the click.”

“But 14 isn’t there.”

“It’s there at 11:40,” Marco said. “That’s the gap. That’s when the building is there.”

The phone went dead at 23:58, and the city made its click, and Dell went to bed.
