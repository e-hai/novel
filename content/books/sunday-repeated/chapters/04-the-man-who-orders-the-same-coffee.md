# The Man Who Orders the Same Coffee

There was a café on the quay with four tables and a woman named Ruth who opened at seven and closed at two, because the quay had no night trade.

Dell had never been in it. On the fifth Sunday he went in, because a man who has been repeating the same day for a month begins to look for the seams in it, and a café with four tables is a good place to look.

There was another man at the window table: sixty or so, a coat that had been good ten years ago, a cup in front of him that he had not touched.

“Coffee,” he said, when Dell came in. “Black. No sugar.”

Ruth wrote it down. She had already written it down four times that morning, and Dell knew that because she had four slips on the spike, all of them identical.

The man looked at Dell and said, “You’re the courier.”

“You’re the man who orders the same coffee.”

“I’m the man who has ordered the same coffee for eleven Sundays.” He pushed the cup an inch. “Sit down. You’re the first person who’s ever noticed.”

His name was Aubrey Tal. He had been a doorkeeper, he said, at a building on Marlow Quay, and he had been doorkeeper on the Sunday in 2014 when the structure was declared unsafe, and he had signed the sheet that let the last two men out and did not sign the sheet that let himself out.

“There were two men inside,” Aubrey said. “I counted two. I signed for two. When the wall came down they found one.”

“Marco.”

“Marco Kwan.” Aubrey turned his cup a quarter turn. “And a room with a door that isn’t a door, that opens for you and closes for me, because I’m the one who didn’t sign.”

“So why am I here?”

“Because you’re the one who can deliver,” Aubrey said. “I’ve had eleven Sundays to work that out. The building opens for nineteen minutes. The parcel has to be signed for by the recipient. The recipient has to be present. The only person who can make all three true at once is a courier who keeps his own bag.”

Ruth came back with a second cup and put it in front of Dell.

“You didn’t order,” she said.

“I didn’t.”

“He did.” She nodded at Aubrey. “He orders two every Sunday. He only drinks one.”
