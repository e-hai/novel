# Sunday, Repeated

The first Sunday, Dell Kwan delivered a parcel to a building that was not there.

The address was on Marlow Quay, number 14, fourth floor. He had been on the quay a hundred times, usually in summer, usually with a coffee, and he knew the buildings the way a courier knows buildings: by lift, by buzzer, by which ones had a guard who would let you in and which ones had a guard who would pretend not to see you.

Number 14 was not one of them. Between 12 and 16 there was a gap with a skip in it and a wall that said DANGER — UNSAFE STRUCTURE in paint that had been white in 2014.

He checked the label twice. Then he called the number on it, and it rang eleven times and stopped.

He wrote *no access* on the scanner, returned the parcel to the depot, and went home.

At 23:59 he was in bed, and at 23:59 the city made a sound he had never heard: not a bang, not a siren. A click, the way a tape clicks when it reaches the end.

The next morning he woke up in the same bed, at the same minute, and the scanner said SUNDAY, and the parcel was in his bag.

He did not understand it at first. Nobody does. He delivered the same sixteen parcels to the same sixteen addresses, and at 23:59 the tape clicked again, and the next morning it was Sunday, and the parcel was in his bag.

On the fourth Sunday he kept the parcel.

He opened it on his kitchen table at midnight, with the tape clicking behind him like a room settling.

Inside was a phone. Not a new one; a phone from 2014, scratched, with a cracked corner and a case that had once been yellow.

It rang at 23:59 exactly, and Dell, who had never been brave in his life, answered it.
