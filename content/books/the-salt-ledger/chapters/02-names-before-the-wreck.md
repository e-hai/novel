# Names Before the Wreck

The harbourmaster came on a Thursday, in a coat too good for the Point.

His name was Halloran. He had signed the inquiry into the *Ardent Goose*, and he had signed the paper that gave Nell’s father a pension and took the light away from her. He stood in the kitchen with his hat in his hands and said he was sorry for her loss, and then he said he had heard she had the book.

“It’s keeper’s business,” Nell said.

“It’s board’s business now.”

She put it on the table and opened it to March. He read the page twice. His face did not change, which told her he had known.

“Your father was ill at the end,” he said.

“He was ill for a year. He wrote every day.”

“Then he wrote badly.”

“He wrote the *Goose* nine days before the *Goose* was lost.” She turned the book toward him. “Twice. Once in February without the bodies, once in March with four. Then he went back and wrote it again on the second of March, so it would look like he knew.”

Halloran looked at the stove, not at her.

“What do you want?” he asked.

“I want to know who told him.”

“Nobody told him.”

“Then who wrote it?”

“The book writes,” he said, and immediately wished he had not, because Nell had spent thirty-one years in a lamp room and knew what a keeper’s book was for. It was for writing, in a hand, in ink, by a person who had stood in the weather.

She closed the ledger. “You can go.”

He did not go. He took a purse from his coat and set it on the table, and the coins made a sound like water on stone.

“The board will pay for the book,” he said. “They’ll pay for the light. They’ll pay for the pension to be quiet. Three hundred. It’s a fair price for a dying man’s mistakes.”

Nell looked at the purse for a long time.

Then she took the ledger to the window, where the last of the light was going down over the Black Shoal, and opened it, and read aloud the newest entry, which had not been there at breakfast.

*1 April. Wind S 5. Light out. Halloran, of the Harbour Board. Ledger closed.*
