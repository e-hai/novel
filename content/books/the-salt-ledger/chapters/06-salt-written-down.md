# Salt, Written Down

The board met in September. Nell went, because a keeper who does not go to the board gets written about by the board.

She had one page, copied out in her own hand, and she read it standing up.

“The book writes before the wreck,” she said. “You know this, because you have been paying me to pretend it doesn’t. I am not asking you to believe it. I am asking you to write down what happens when it is closed: the entries stop, the bell stops, and the next time a cutter passes the Point in the dark, nobody knows whether it will come in.”

Halloran sat with his hands folded.

“And if it stays open?”

“Then every entry has to be read. Every year. By two people, one of them the keeper, one of them from this board, and both of them signing that they read it.”

The board argued for an hour, which is short, as boards go. In the end they passed a resolution with eleven words in it and a great deal of paper around the words.

*Ledger to remain open. Annual reading. Two signatures, before the ink.*

Nell took the resolution home and pinned it inside the cover of the book, where her father had pinned nothing.

Then she wrote, on the first blank page, in a hand that shook only at the last word:

*The book is kept open by people who will read it. One signature is a seal. Two signatures are a promise. Whoever keeps this light next: read the year, and sign it before the ink dries, and if you cannot sign it, do not close it.*

The bell rang at seven that evening, once.

Nell went out with the lamp lit and the wind at her back. On the step, in the last of the light, the ledger lay open at a new entry in a hand she did not know and had not written.

*25 September. Wind SW 5. Light steady. Rourke, of the Point. Book open. Signature: one. Second: to come.*

She stood there a long time, with the sea below and the light turning above, and then she went inside and put the kettle on, because the second signature was not going to write itself, and because somebody had to be there when it arrived.
