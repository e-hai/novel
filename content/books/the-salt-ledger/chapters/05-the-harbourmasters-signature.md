# The Harbourmaster's Signature

Halloran came back in June with two men and a paper.

The paper said the board had reviewed the keeper’s book and found it irregular. It said the book would be closed, sealed, and deposited with the harbour office, and that the Point would be tended by a mechanician until a new keeper was appointed. It had three signatures on it. His was the third.

Nell read it twice at the door.

“You’ll want the book,” she said.

“I’ll want the book.”

“And the light.”

“The light goes to the mechanician.”

She stepped aside and let them in, which surprised him enough that he checked the step before crossing it.

The ledger was on the kitchen table, open, the latest entry wet.

*14 June. Wind W 2. Light steady. Halloran, of the Board. Book closed. Signature: one.*

He read it and went grey around the mouth.

“You wrote that.”

“I didn’t.”

“It’s your hand.”

“It’s my hand,” Nell said. “The book has learned it. It learned my father’s the same way. It writes what is about to be true, so the board can say afterwards that someone knew.”

Halloran’s men stood by the door with their hats in their hands.

“Sign it,” he said. “Sign the closure and we’ll go.”

“I’ll sign it,” she said, “when there are two signatures, and both of them belong to people who will have to read this book again in a year.”

“That is not the procedure.”

“Then the procedure is wrong.” She turned the ledger to face him. “You want the book closed, and a closed book is a book nobody reads. You want that because it is the only way this page stops being a record of you.”

He looked at the page for a long moment. Then he took the pen, and wrote his name under the entry, and dated it, and pushed the paper toward her.

“Two signatures,” he said. “Mine, and yours.”

Nell took the pen out of his hand and did not write.

“Two signatures,” she said, “and one of them has to be a person who intends to keep reading.”

Outside, the tide was coming in over the Black Shoal, and the bell rang once, though there was no wind.
