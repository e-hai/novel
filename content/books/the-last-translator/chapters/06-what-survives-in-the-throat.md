# What Survives in the Throat

A year later the Bureau came back, which is what bureaus do, and this time the form was different.

It was called a *Language Continuity Registration*, and it asked for the names of speakers, the domains of use, and a plan for instruction. It offered a small grant and a room in a school and the loan of a recorder.

The officer who brought it was not the officer from before. This one was younger and had read the appendix, and said so, and said that the Bureau had decided it was cheaper to register a language than to keep denying one.

Sefa read the form twice and put it on the table.

“It asks for a licensed translator to sign,” she said.

“It asks for you.”

“It asks me to state that the language is continuing.”

“It does.”

She thought about the market, and the laundry, and the nine people, and the fire that burned clean in the yard behind the customs house. She thought about the imperfect, which is the tense you use when the bread is rising, and when the child is asleep and you are not sure he will stay asleep, and when somebody is dead and you are still talking to them.

“I’ll sign,” she said, “if the form says one more thing.”

The officer took out a pen. “What thing?”

“That it was broken, and that we broke it, and that it is being repaired.”

The young officer wrote it in the margin, in his own hand, because the form had no box for it.

Then Sefa signed her name under the sentence, and dated it, and added, in Vesh, in the imperfect, where the Bureau’s language could not follow her:

*It is being spoken. It is being taught. It is not finished.*
