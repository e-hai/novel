# The Certification Hearing

The hearing was in a room with windows, which Sefa took as a good sign and later understood was a mistake.

There were three commissioners and a clerk and the officer who had hired her. The Bureau’s grammarian sat at the far end of the table with a folder and a pen and the expression of a man who has been asked to attend something beneath him.

The clerk read the certification aloud: no living speakers, no untranslated literature, no continuing use in any domain.

Then Sefa stood up with her appendix.

She did not read it. She read four sentences from it, and then she asked the grammarian a question.

“In the 2019 edition, the imperfect aspect was removed. Is that correct?”

The grammarian looked at the officer. The officer looked at the clerk. The clerk looked at the folder.

“The 2019 edition was a simplification,” the grammarian said.

“Is that a yes?”

“It is a simplification.”

“It is a yes,” the commissioner in the middle said. “Answer the question.”

The grammarian answered it. Then he answered eleven more, and by the fourth one he had stopped looking at the officer, and by the eighth he had started reading from his own folder, and by the eleventh the room had understood that the Bureau had not certified a dead language. It had certified a language it had broken.

“The certification before us,” Sefa said, “requires me to state that Vesh has no continuing use in any domain. I cannot state that. I can state that Vesh has no continuing use in any domain *that the Bureau permits*. Those are different sentences, and one of them is a lie, and I am the licensed translator, and the lie would be mine.”

She put the appendix on the table.

“I will sign a certification that says the language is injured. I will sign a certification that lists what was removed and who removed it. I will not sign that it is dead, because it is not dead, and because in nine days’ time a child in this district will say a banned word to her mother and her mother will understand her.”

The middle commissioner wrote something down.

“That is not a certification the Bureau can accept,” she said.

“I know,” Sefa said. “I’m learning that phrase.”
