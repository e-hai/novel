# Two Hands for One Man

Entry 92 — 28 April. To read it legally, someone must hold the second hand who is not Mara and not in her shop.

That leaves the second inspector, who is licensed to witness but not to repair; Wren, who is an apprentice and cannot sign; and a shop in the northern ring that has never answered a letter from Cinder Lane.

The northern shop answered in six days. Their second hand is a man of seventy who has been a repairman for forty years and has not run a loom since the licence changed, and he has agreed to read a core and not to touch it.

The arrangement took two months and three letters: the inspector attests the reading, Wren transcribes, the northern second hand holds the frame, and I sit in the chair where the customer sits.

Four people, one core, one man's week. Mara has written the conditions into the ledger herself, which makes entry 92 the second page in this book in her hand.
