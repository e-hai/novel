# The Apprentice

Entry 31 — 3 March. Wren Adey came with a letter from a shop in the northern ring that closed in the autumn and a bag with two cores in it that belong to her mother.

She is nineteen. She has been at a bench for three years, which is two more than I had at her age, and she can read a beat off a core faster than I can, which I found irritating and then useful.

The letter says she is competent, honest, and difficult about hours. The last part turned out to mean that she arrives at seven and leaves at seven and will not pretend either hour is a favour.

I took her on for the reading, not the bench. Two accounts per core means twice the reading, and the reading is the part I have been doing badly since November. Mara signed the hire on the same day, with one line under it: she is not the second account. Mara is.
