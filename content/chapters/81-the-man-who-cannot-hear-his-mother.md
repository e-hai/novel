# The Man Who Cannot Hear His Mother

Entry 81 — 10 February. The first was a man of sixty-one who had a bridge removed four years ago: his mother's voice, gone by request, with a single signature because the rule did not exist yet.

He came back in February and said he had changed his mind. Not for the reason I expected — he did not want the voice for himself. He wanted it for a granddaughter who is six and will not remember her great-grandmother and has nobody to hear her speak.

The core is intact, the bridge is stable, and the voice is not in it. We can reconstruct the shape and we cannot return the sound, because the trade's bridging removes the sound before it removes the words.

He sat in the front room for a while and then said the thing that opens Part XII: the record is fine. The record says I asked for it. The record does not say what it costs.

I have written the whole of his sentence in the entry, because it is the argument the rest of the book is built on and because he is not wrong.
