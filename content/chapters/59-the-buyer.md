# The Buyer

Entry 59 — 5 September. A customer came in to buy fill, not a repair, which the shop does not do.

He wanted two hundred hours of childhood — any childhood, his word — and he was willing to pay the warehouse price plus the shop's margin. He is forty-eight, he has a good job, and he told me what he wanted it for without being asked: he is writing a memoir and the early chapters are thin.

I said the shop does not sell fill, only repairs, and that the difference is not a formality. He said he had bought fill in the northern ring and had been satisfied, and that he had come to Cinder Lane because he had heard the shop was honest and he wanted to know what honest would say.

What honest said: a memoir with borrowed childhood in it is a false record, and the falsity is not in the writing, it is in the reader. He thanked me, which I did not expect, and left, and I have written the whole conversation into the ledger because it is the only entry in which the shop was asked a moral question and answered it in one sentence.
