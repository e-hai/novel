# The Refusal

Entry 23 — 21 November. A father came in with his son's core and a request I have heard four times this year: take out the hospital.

The boy was nine. He had been in a ward for eleven weeks and the core held the corridor, the ceiling tiles, the taste of the water. The father wanted it gone so the boy could go back to school in the spring and not be the child who was ill.

Mara read it and would not sign. Not because the repair was impossible — it is the easiest repair in the trade — but because the second account, the boy's, had not been asked. He was nine. He had a second account anyway, and it said he wanted to keep the ceiling tiles because he had counted them.

We refused. The father shouted for a while and then sat down and asked what a refusal was, exactly. I said it was a record. He said that was a poor answer. It is, but it is the true one.
