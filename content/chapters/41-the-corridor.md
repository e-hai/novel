# The Corridor

Entry 41 — 4 May. The head of a school on the eastern edge called the shop because a corridor had gone quiet.

Not silent. Quiet: the sound in it arrived late, by about a third of a second, so that footsteps reached you after the person did. Nine children had mentioned it, two had started going the long way, and one had stopped coming in by that door at all.

Nothing in that corridor has ever been repaired. There is no core, no customer, no seam. It is a building, and a building is not a patient, and the trade has no procedure for a building.

I went anyway, with a pressure gauge and no instrument that measures anything. The corridor was cold at the north end, and the acoustics were wrong in the specific way that a room is wrong when something heavy has been removed from under the floor.

I wrote four lines and declined the case. The head asked what she should do. I said: keep a record, and she looked at me as though I had told her to pray.
