# The Hours

Entry 70 — 21 November. The column of hours that cannot be billed is now eleven months long and it is the only number in this book that a stranger could check.

Two thousand one hundred and forty hours. Reading, refusals, the four silent days, the second readings that changed a decision, the tests on nine lots, the four addresses I walked.

The Ward's examiner read it in February and wrote that it was not a defect. Mara says that is the most the office can say and the least it should. Wren says it is the shop's real product, and that the counter card should say so, and I have not put it on the counter card.

What I have done is start a second column: hours that were billed for work that was not reading. It is nearly empty, and it will stay nearly empty, and the emptiness is the only thing in this book that resembles an answer.

End of Part X. Nine dual runs, one drift, one refusal in writing, four silent days, and a column of two thousand one hundred and forty hours.
