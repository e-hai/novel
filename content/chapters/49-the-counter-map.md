# The Counter Map

Entry 49 — 30 June. The list is a map now. Wren drew it on the back of the guidance, four streets, sixty-one addresses, one pin per three instances, and left it on the counter overnight because the counter is the only flat surface in the shop.

A customer saw it at nine in the morning. A man of about forty with a core in a case, who had come in for a bridge and left without one — not because the repair was refused, but because he stood at the counter and read the map and asked whether his street was on it.

It was. He put the core back in the case and said he would rather keep the damage than be a pin. I have no procedure for that. The trade is built on the assumption that people want the damage gone.

I took the map down and put it in the ledger, folded, and the fold is why the entry is short. Mara says a map on a counter is an argument. A map in a ledger is evidence. She is right, and the shop is now keeping evidence where customers cannot see it, which is a thing I have criticised other shops for.
