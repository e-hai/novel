# The Witness

Entry 89 — 7 April. A solicitor wrote to the shop asking whether it would attest a record: a family's account of a fire, kept in three cores, taken at three shops, disagreeing about the hour.

The shop's licence does not cover attestation. The trade attests repairs, not events, and the Ward has no category for a shop that says a family's record is internally consistent.

Mara said no. Wren said the ledger already attests to more than repairs — a hundred and four addresses, a notebook, a list of nine children. I said yes, and the yes is the first thing in this book that the shop has done outside its licence.

Four hours of reading, three accounts, one signature each, and a page that says: the three accounts agree about the fire and disagree about the hour, and the disagreement is in the memories of three people who were in the room, and the shop has not repaired any of them.

The solicitor filed it. Nothing came of it. The entry is long and the fee is a pound, and it is the only paid work in Part XII.

You are reading a ledger written by a shop that no longer knows what it is licensed to do.
