# The Board Letter

Entry 74 — 16 December. The board wanted a finding by the twenty-first. I sent a page of questions.

Nine questions, each about a page of Ader's file, each answerable by yes or no or by a page number. The board's clerk wrote back asking whether the shop declined to make a finding, and I wrote back that the shop declined to make a finding about a man on the basis of a file that cannot say who was in the room.

That is a finding, in the sense that it is a decision about evidence. It is not a finding in the sense the board uses, which requires a category, and the categories available are defect, category, and no defect.

Wren asked whether we had just refused to do the work. I said we had done the work and refused the format, and that the difference would matter to a reader in fifty years and not at all to the clerk. She wrote it in the ledger as the shop's first refusal of a format.

The board's reply came on the twenty-second and asked for the questions in a form.
