# The Inspector's Loom

The inspector came on the eighth day, which left two, and he came with a loom.

It was smaller than Elias's. It came in two cases, one for the frame and one for the plate, and the inspector carried them himself rather than letting the courier do it. He set them down inside the door, took off his coat, folded it over the back of the one chair, and said, "Ader. I'm not here to close you."

"That's what people say before they close you."

"It is," Ader agreed. "I'll say it once more and then we can work. I'm not here to close you. I'm here to write a finding. There's a difference, and it's the only difference my job has ever had."

He was about fifty, with the flat hands of someone who had done bench work and the careful shoes of someone who had stopped. He assembled his loom on the counter without asking, and it fitted, because the counter had been built to a standard that predated the standard.

"You'll want the log," Elias said.

"I'll want the log at the end. First I want to see a repair."

"There's nothing booked till four."

"Then I'll wait." Ader sat on the stool. "Or you could show me the one you did. The one with the seam."

Elias looked at him for a moment. Then he went to the drawer, and it caught, and he lifted the front edge and cleared it, and he took out the mother's core and set it on the bench between them.

Ader did not reach for it. He looked at the sleeve, at the label gone soft at the corner, and he said, "You kept the client's core."

"He didn't take it back."

"Most of them don't." Ader turned the core over with one finger. "Do you know what my job is, Mr Vane? Not the title. The work."

"You audit repairs."

"I audit the difference between a repair and a record of a repair. Ninety-nine times out of a hundred there is no difference, because the loom writes what the loom did. The hundredth time, there is a difference, and it is usually a person."

He opened his case and took out a plate, and the plate was a core, and he seated it in his own loom. The frame lit.

"What's that?" Elias asked.

"My review. The one before yours." Ader's hands moved on the plate. "I have to write a finding on you, and the finding has to be defensible, and I have spent six days reading your file and I cannot tell whether you were careful or lucky. That is not a finding. That is a mood."

The scene on his loom resolved. Elias saw a room with three chairs and a table, and a man at the table, and the man was Ader, younger, in a different coat, and the man was holding a paper.

"I'm repairing my own doubt," Ader said, without embarrassment. "It's allowed. It's in the manual, under the section nobody reads. An auditor may submit to a repair of an auditor's doubt, provided the repair is logged and the log is shown to the reviewed party. Here is my log."

He turned the frame so Elias could see the small screen at the side, where the loom had written, in its flat official hand: *Doubt — whether finding is fair. Repair: fill from the auditor's own record of a fair finding. Result: provisional.*

"Provisional," Elias said.

"There is no other result. If I repaired it to certainty I would be lying to myself in a way that would show." Ader sat back. "Now. Show me the seam."

So Elias seated the mother's core and ran it to the last intact frame, and then he stopped, and showed the inspector the place where the sound track went pale and where he had set the narrow note along the edge.

Ader watched it four times. He did not ask what the note said. He asked, "Could you have closed it?"

"Yes."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because the client could tell me what she said, and the machine couldn't."

Ader nodded slowly, and something in his face changed by a very small amount, the way a room changes when a lamp is turned down.

"That," he said, "is a finding."

He took out his notebook and wrote, and Elias could not see the words, only the length of them, and the length was short.

"You left a seam," Ader said. "That's a finding."

"It's a reading."

"Then I'll write it as a reading, and I'll note that it was found in a seam." He closed the notebook. "You'll have the report in a week. Don't take the sign down again. It makes the file look like it's hiding."
