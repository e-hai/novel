# The Ledger Read Aloud

Entry 86 — 17 March. We read the ledger aloud, all hundred and sixty entries to date, over three evenings, in the front room, with the door locked.

It was Wren's idea and it was a review, and the review found four things the pages alone had not: that the shop says a customer is right eleven times and never says why; that the word defect appears forty-one times and the word person four; that every entry about the grey ends in an address, which is a habit and not a finding; and that the shop has never once recorded what it did with the money it did not charge.

The fourth one is the one that stayed with me. Two thousand hours unbilled, and no page that says what the shop lived on instead.

Mara's line, at the end of the third evening: a ledger that cannot account for its own survival is a ledger written by someone who expects to be rescued. I wrote that down after the reading, in the book, which makes entry 86 the only entry written about an entry.

The shop is not being rescued. The bank has written twice.
