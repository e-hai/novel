# The Man with Two Mothers

Entry 65 — 17 October. A man came in with two accounts of his mother and asked for one to be taken out.

The accounts were not a dual repair. They were two separate cores, taken at two shops, four years apart, and they disagree about whether his mother had a garden. In one she does, in the other she does not, and he has been living with both since 2019.

He wanted the garden removed. He said the garden was the false one and he could not prove it, and that living with two mothers was worse than living with a bad one.

The rule says a shop may reconcile two accounts and may not delete one, because deleting an account is a repair of the customer's record and not of the customer's memory. I explained that, and he said the distinction was the trade's, not his.

We did nothing. He paid nothing. The entry has his sentence in it, quoted exactly, because it is the best argument against the rule that anyone has made in this book.
