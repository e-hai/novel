# The Houses on the List

Entry 46 — 9 June. I took the list and walked it.

Four addresses, one afternoon, no appointment and no customer. A house with a hedge, a flat above a shop, a bungalow with a ramp, a cottage that had been re-roofed. In each one somebody answered the door and in each one the conversation went the same way: yes, something is wrong with the street; no, we have not reported it; no, we do not want to be on a list.

The absence is not the same in each of them. In the house with the hedge it is the garden — nobody can say what was planted in it. In the flat it is a stair. In the bungalow it is the hour the nurse comes. In the cottage it is the roof, which was replaced and is remembered as never having been replaced.

Four different absences with one shape: an event that happened and left no viewpoint. That is the office's definition, not mine, and I did not enjoy finding it in four houses on a Tuesday.
