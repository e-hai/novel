# The Waiting Room

Entry 43 — 19 May. A clinic in the district has a waiting room where the grey is doing something the trade has no word for.

Patients who sit in it for more than ten minutes come out unable to say how long they waited. Not confused — accurate about everything else, sometimes more accurate — but the duration is simply absent, in the way a word is absent when you have forgotten it.

The clinic likes this. It has thirty-two chairs and a queue that used to be a complaint and is now a silence. The administrator asked me whether it could be extended to the corridor outside the treatment rooms.

I said no, and gave the reason in writing: a memory that is absent is not a service, and the trade does not sell absence, it repairs damage.

He said the difference was a formality and that I was the only person in the district who still believed in it. I wrote that down too. It is the first entry in the book where the shop's own opinion is quoted by somebody else.
