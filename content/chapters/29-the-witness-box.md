# The Witness Box

Entry 29 — 11 February. Summons, one page, for the district session on the twenty-third.

The Ward wants the shop's ledger. Not the shop, the ledger: they asked for the book, in a box, with both hands available to attest the pages. Mara says this is the first sensible thing the Ward has asked for in two years, because a ledger can be checked and a repairman cannot.

I have been keeping the book for three months. It has forty-one entries, eleven refusals, one repair that was correct and wrong, and a column of hours that cannot be billed. I read it through before I boxed it, which is the first time I have read it as a document rather than as a diary.

Twelve pages in, I found something I had written on the eighteenth of November and forgotten: two hands cost a day. The Ward will read that line and draw a conclusion about the shop's capacity, and the conclusion will be correct, and I do not want them to have it.
