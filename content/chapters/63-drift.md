# Drift

Entry 63 — 3 October. The first legal dual-account repair drifted.

Six weeks after the run, both accounts moved by about four minutes: not the content, the position. The customer said she now remembered the afternoon happening later in the day, and both cores agreed with her, which is the correct behaviour of a memory and the wrong behaviour of a record.

A single-account repair does not drift, because a single account has nothing to drift against. A dual repair has two frames and a seam between them, and the seam is where the drift accumulates, the way a joint in a road takes the cracks.

Mara says this is not a defect. She says it is what a memory does when two people have agreed about it, and that the trade's thirty years of single-account repair were spent producing memories that could not be shared.

I have written her sentence into the ledger and marked it, in the margin, as an opinion rather than a finding, because I do not know yet whether she is right.
