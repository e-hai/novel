# Two Hands on the Joint

Entry 66 — 24 October. A joint signature attests that a second person read the account before the loom. It does not attest that the repair was right.

This is the gap that Part VI opened and Part X closes. The Ward's rule assumes that two people reading an account will catch what one person would miss, which is true for damage and false for judgement: a repair can be technically perfect and humanly wrong, and two signatures will not catch that.

Mara put it in the ledger as a line: a joint is a check on the reading and not on the decision. I put a line under it: then the decision is where the shop is unlicensed.

The office has no procedure for an unlicensed part of a licensed trade. Neither does the shop. The difference is that the shop writes it down every week and the office writes it down once a year.

Wren, who has been quiet for a month, asked whether that was why the book got shorter after the hearing. It is. The entries are shorter because the sentences are.
