# Reading It

Entry 93 — 5 May. I read it. Nine minutes, 2015, a shop on Cinder Lane.

The room is the front room, before it was the front room — the old counter, the window that did not open, a woman standing where the ledger shelf is now. She is holding a cloth, and the cloth is not the one I have been keeping in a drawer.

And I am not in the room. The account is hers, seen from the chair, and the man at the bench in it has my hands and a face I do not recognise.

That is not a defect. An engram records a viewpoint, and a viewpoint is where the person was, and in 2015 I was at the bench with my back turned, which is why the face is not in the account.

The northern second hand read it twice and said: it is complete, it is accurate, and it does not contain you. He said it kindly, which was worse. I have written his sentence down and I have not written down mine.
