# What a Seam Is

Entry 32 — 9 March. Teaching is a review with a witness.

I told Wren what a seam is: not the join, the place where the memory stops agreeing with itself. A good seam is invisible from inside and faintly warm from outside, like a wall that has been repainted. A bad seam is a beat — a room arriving before its own sound.

She asked when a seam is a defect and when it is a category. Nobody has asked me that since the hearing, and I gave her the answer the Ward gives, which is that it depends on the second account. She wrote it down. Then she asked whether that meant the rule was about people or about paperwork.

I said people. She wrote that down too, and I could see her deciding whether to believe it, which is the correct thing for an apprentice to do with a sentence from a man who has been reviewed twice.
