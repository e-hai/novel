# Reading a Reviewer

Entry 72 — 2 December. Ader's accounts disagree with each other about the day he inspected this shop.

In one he came on a Tuesday in March and the door was open. In another he came on a Thursday and had to knock. The shop's own ledger has him on the nineteenth, which was a Thursday, and the door was locked because we were in the back with a core.

That is not a defect. It is what a memory does when a man has been in ninety shops and the trade has taught him to distrust his own record.

The defect is smaller and worse: in all three accounts he is alone. He was not alone. Wren was at the front desk and signed him in, and her signature is on the page, and in his file there is no second person in the room at any point.

A reviewer who cannot remember a witness is a reviewer whose findings cannot be witnessed. That sentence is mine, in this ledger, and I have not sent it anywhere, and I know what that makes me.
