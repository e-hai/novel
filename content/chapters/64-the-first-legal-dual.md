# The First Legal Dual

Entry 64 — 10 October. We ran a dual repair under licence, joint named before the loom, both accounts attested, both names in the ledger.

The customer was a man of thirty-four who had two accounts of his mother's death: one from the week it happened and one from four years later, taken at a different shop. The two accounts disagreed about the room, the hour, and whether the machine was on.

We ran the frames together for nine minutes and produced a single account that both hands signed for. The customer came out quiet and said it was better, and then asked a question I could not answer: which of the two had been repaired.

Neither. Both. The run does not repair an account; it reconciles a pair, and what it produces is a third thing that belongs to the customer and to nobody's record.

Mara wrote the entry's last line and I let her, which is the only time in this book that a page is in her hand and not mine: two accounts, one signature each, and no name for what we made.
