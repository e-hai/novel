# The Shop as Record

Entry 90 — 14 April. The shop is a file now. That is the plainest sentence in this book and it has taken ninety entries to become writable.

A bench repairs and keeps accounts. A file attests, refuses, compares, and holds what customers bring in that cannot be repaired: notebooks, lists, letters, a family's fire, a corridor with nine children.

The Ward has no category for a file. The trade's licence covers cores. The shop's income comes from cores. Ninety per cent of what the shop has done since November has been reading, and reading is not on the licence, and the office has never asked.

Mara put the question to me at the end of April: if the shop is a file, what is the ledger? I said the ledger is the file's record of itself. She said then the ledger is the only licensed thing in the room.

I have written that sentence at the top of Part XIII, under the date, in my own hand. It is the last sentence I write before the last part of the book, and I have not decided yet whether it is a boast or an admission.

End of Part XII. Nine testimonies, one pound, three accounts of a fire, and a shop that is a file with a bench in it.
