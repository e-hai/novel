# Two Hands

Entry 22 — 18 November. We did the arithmetic on the joint rule and it is not cheap.

A single repair takes a morning. A dual-account repair takes a morning for the bench and most of an afternoon for the reading: two accounts, two histories of the same room, the places where they disagree, and a sheet of paper that has to be filled in before either hand touches the loom. Yesterday we spent four hours on a customer whose core was eleven minutes long.

So the shop raised its price and wrote a line on the counter explaining why. Two customers read it and left. One stayed, a woman who wanted a wedding removed and had been quoted less somewhere else, and she stayed because the other shop had not asked her whether the second account existed.

Mara says the price is the honesty, not the repair. I am not sure I would put it that way. But the ledger now has a column for hours that cannot be billed, and it is not an empty column.
