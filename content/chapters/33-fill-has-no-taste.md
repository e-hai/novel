# Fill Has No Taste

Entry 33 — 14 March. Fill, taught in one afternoon.

Fill is what holds a memory together when the middle is gone. It is not invented: it is borrowed, weighed, and stamped, and it has no taste, which is the trade's way of saying that it carries no feeling of its own. A customer who has been filled remembers the shape of an afternoon and cannot say what the light was like.

Wren asked whether that was a kindness. I said it was a service. She asked whether the difference was the price. I said the difference was whether the customer was told, and she looked at the ledger, which is now the thing in this shop that answers questions.

The rest of the lesson was technical: how much fill for how much gap, how to keep the donor's weather out of the buyer's room, how to write the lot number. She learned it in an hour and a half. I learned it in a year, at a bench, from a man who is dead.
