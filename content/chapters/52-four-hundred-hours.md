# Four Hundred Hours

Entry 52 — 21 July. Lot 8841, four hundred hours, stamped the third of June, priced at nine hundred and forty.

Four hundred hours of somebody's back garden in a summer when the garden was new. There is a wedding in it that the donor did not attend, a dog that the donor did not own, and a fortnight at a coast the donor has never seen — because donors are paid by the hour and hours are hours, and nobody at the weighbridge checks whether the hours are the donor's own.

Wren read the lot sheet and asked what we would do with a wedding. I said we would bridge a customer's torn memory with four minutes of somebody else's party, and that this is legal, and that she should write down how it made her feel rather than what she thought.

She wrote: it is like borrowing a photograph and being told the people in it loved you. That is the best sentence in the ledger this year and it is in the apprentice's hand.
