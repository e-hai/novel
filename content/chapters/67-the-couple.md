# The Couple

Entry 67 — 31 October. A couple brought one wedding and two accounts of it.

They had been married eleven years, and the accounts had drifted apart in the ordinary way until they no longer agreed about the band, the rain, or whether the cake was cut before the speeches. Neither wanted a dual run. Both wanted their own account confirmed.

This is the case the trade has no instrument for: two customers, one event, and a request that the shop certify a winner. The rule says reconcile or refuse, and reconciliation here would mean producing a third wedding that neither of them attended.

We refused, in writing, and gave the reason as: a shared memory can be reconciled and cannot be arbitrated. They read it in the front room and then asked whether we would do it anyway for the price of two.

I said no twice. Mara said the second no was for free and should have been in the ledger, and it is now, under her hand, which makes October the month with two hands in it on purpose.
