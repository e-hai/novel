# The Ledger Changes Hands

Entry 40 — 30 April. From this week Wren keeps one page in seven.

It was her suggestion, made in the way apprentices make suggestions, which is to say as a question about whether it would be inefficient. A ledger written by one hand is a diary. A ledger written by two is a record, because the second hand can be asked what the first one meant.

Her pages are shorter than mine and more exact. She writes the hour a customer arrived, the hour the decision was made, the hour the loom ran, and she leaves a space at the bottom of every page for the thing she could not put in a column.

I read her first page and found a note in that space: the customer's sister called on the telephone and asked whether the repair had been done, and Wren had said yes, and the sister had not known there was a repair.

I do not know what to do with that note. It is in the book now, in a hand that is not mine, and it will be read by whoever reads this ledger in fifty years.

End of Part VII. One hundred and four hours of unbillable reading, one error of record, and a second hand that writes better notes than mine.
