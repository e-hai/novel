# The Second Rule

Entry 21 — 14 November. The shop reopened on a Monday, and the log was mine from the first line.

The rule now reads: two hands on every core, the joint named before the loom, both names in the ledger whether the repair is done or refused. Mara signs beside me. She does not sign as a witness. She signs as the second account, which is a heavier thing, and she has made it clear she intends to be inconvenient about it.

The first customer was a man of about sixty with a core that had been repaired twice already, once well and once by somebody who wanted the case closed before Friday. The damage was a beat: a room that arrived half a second ahead of the sound in it. I could have bridged it in an hour. Mara read the second account, put her pen down, and said the customer had not been told what the bridge would do to the rest of the room.

He had not. She was right. I refused the repair and wrote the refusal in the ledger in my own hand, and the shop lost the fee, and that is the first entry of the second half of the book.
