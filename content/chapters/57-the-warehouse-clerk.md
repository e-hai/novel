# The Warehouse Clerk

Entry 57 — 25 August. The clerk at the weighbridge has a notebook.

Six years, ninety forms a day, and a line per form: date, lot, donor's first name, and a letter. The letters are her own code: F for freely given, D for doubtful, N for not seen. In six years she has written N four hundred and eleven times and never once written it on a form.

She showed me the notebook because I asked her how she slept. She said she slept badly and that the notebook was the reason she could still sleep at all, and that she had been waiting six years for somebody to ask.

I have never been handed evidence by a stranger before. The trade has no procedure for it. I photocopied nothing — a copy would put her name in a file — and wrote an entry that says: a record exists, held by a person, not in the ledger, and the shop has seen it.

Mara read the entry and said: that is not evidence, that is a witness. I said the same thing, and neither of us wrote it down.
