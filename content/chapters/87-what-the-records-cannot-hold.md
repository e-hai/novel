# What the Records Cannot Hold

Entry 87 — 24 March. A list of things the ledger cannot hold, written at the end of a week in which four customers told us things we did not write down.

That a repair worked and made a life smaller. That a seam held and a man did not. That a boy climbs a staircase he asked to lose. That a corridor with nine children in it is not a building.

Four lines, and each one is a testimony, and the ledger has a column for testimony now, and the column has no numbers in it, and the four lines are not in it either, because a testimony given in a front room is not the same as a testimony entered.

This distinction is the trade's oldest and least examined: what is written is evidence, what is said is weather. The office is built on the first and lives inside the second.

Wren asked whether the shop should keep the four lines anyway. I said yes, and then did not, and the entry records both the yes and the not-doing, which is the only way I know to keep a page honest.
