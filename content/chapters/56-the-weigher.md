# The Weigher

Entry 56 — 18 August. I went back to the warehouse and asked the weigher what a brick is.

He said a brick is four hours and a stamp, and that the hours come from donors who sign at the gate, and that most of them are people who needed money in a week when the money was short. He said the hours are not checked against the donor's own life because there is no instrument for that, and because checking would cost more than the discount.

I asked whether he thought the summer had been sold twice. He said he thought the summer had been sold three times and that one of the lots had gone to a shop in the northern ring that does not ask questions.

He said this the way a man describes weather. That is the part I have written down in full, because it is the only place in the book where somebody tells the truth without being asked and without wanting anything.
